How to Reduce Your Import Duty Legally: Preference and FTA Basics

Practical guidance from HS Code Toolbox

There's a completely legal way to pay less duty, and plenty of exporters never use it because nobody walked them through it. It comes down to two words: preferential origin.

What preference actually means

Duty isn't always the full rate on the schedule. When goods qualify under a free trade agreement — or a country's preferential scheme — they can get a reduced rate or even zero. The product doesn't change. What changes is where it came from and whether that route has a deal.

Where the deals are

Every market has its own web of agreements. The EU, the US and China each run their own sets with different partners. The same product could be duty-free from one source country and fully taxed from another. The benefiting route depends on the agreement and on the product meeting its rules of origin.

The rule of origin is the gatekeeper

Here's the part that trips most people up. Qualifying isn't automatic — the goods have to meet the agreement's specific origin rule. That usually means either the product is wholly obtained in the partner country, or it has undergone enough processing there to count as originating. "Enough" is defined by the agreement, and it varies.

You need the paperwork to claim it

A preference is only real if you can prove it. That means a certificate of origin, or a preferential origin declaration, issued and kept correctly. Ship without it and you pay the full rate — even if the goods would have qualified. The paperwork is the difference between a discount and full price.

The smart move is to check two things before you book: what agreement covers your route, and whether your goods meet its origin rule. From there, the claim is mostly paperwork.
