Commercial Invoice Template — Every Field, Explained

Practical guidance from HS Code Toolbox

The commercial invoice is the backbone document of any export. It drives the HS code, the duty, the value declaration and the clearance itself. Get a field wrong and the shipment can sit at customs while it is fixed. Here is a clean, fillable template with what each line really does.

The template

COMMERCIAL INVOICE Invoice No.: ____ · Date: ____

Bank or agent to be notified on arrival

Why each field matters for customs

  • Description of goods is the field customs reads against your HS code. Vague words like "accessories", "parts" or "general merchandise" are now high-risk triggers for automated checks. Say exactly what it is, and what it is made of.
  • Value must match what you actually got paid or expect to be paid. Under-valuing to "save" duty is the fastest way to a valuation check and a demurrage bill that dwarfs the saving.
  • Country of origin decides which trade agreements apply and can trigger anti-dumping duties. It is not the country you ship from — it is where the goods were manufactured.
  • Terms of sale tell every party who pays freight, insurance and duty. Get this wrong and the responsibility (and cost) lands in the wrong place.

Three rules to avoid a hold

  • Keep the HS code, product description and the packing list consistent on every document — invoice, packing list, bill of lading.
  • Quote exact quantities — do not round "for convenience" — so customs sees one truthful, consistent story.
  • Attach the packing list. Most clearance delays come from a missing or mismatched companion document, not the invoice itself.
