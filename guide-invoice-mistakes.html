What Not to Put on a Commercial Invoice: 7 Mistakes That Trigger Inspections

Practical guidance from HS Code Toolbox

The commercial invoice is the one document customs reads first. Get it sloppy and you buy yourself an inspection, a delay, sometimes a penalty. These seven mistakes are the ones that get shipments pulled.

1. Vague descriptions

"Spare parts" or "miscellaneous goods" tells customs nothing, and they punish it with an exam. Describe the product properly and specifically — what it is, what it's made of, what it does.

2. A description that fights the code

When the words say one thing and the HS code points elsewhere, that's a red flag by design. Customs wants the description and the code to agree. Make them match before you file, not after.

3. Wrong declared value

Duty is calculated on the invoice value. Undervalue it to look cheap and you risk a re-valuation and penalties. The real cost is never worth the saving.

4. Mismatched weights and measures

The invoice should line up with the packing list and the bill of lading. If the weight, carton count or CBM doesn't match across documents, it looks like something is being hidden. Keep the numbers consistent everywhere.

5. Missing or wrong Incoterms

The terms of sale tell customs who pays for what, and it affects the valuation. Leave them off or state them wrong and the declaration is incomplete. State the Incoterm and the named place clearly.

6. No seller or buyer details

Customs needs to know who shipped it and who's receiving it. Incomplete or mismatched party details are a quick way to stall clearance. Keep buyer and seller information full and matching your contracts.

7. Anything that doesn't belong on the invoice

Keep marketing talk, vague promises and unrequested extras off the commercial invoice. It's a legal document, not a pitch. Stick to the facts customs needs.

The invoice is the first impression your shipment makes at the border. Fix these seven and you've removed most of the reasons it gets pulled aside.
