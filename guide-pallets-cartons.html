Pallets vs Cartons: Loading Strategies That Maximize Space

Practical guidance from HS Code Toolbox

At some point every exporter faces this: palletize the cartons, or load them loose? The answer depends on how much you're moving, how the goods handle, and what's waiting at the other end.

Why people palletize in the first place

Pallets make unloading simple. A forklift pulls out one unit at a time instead of someone hand-unloading carton by carton. That speed matters at the destination, where labor and wait time are money. Palletized freight also travels more predictably and keeps cargo cleaner off the floor.

The catch: pallets eat volume

Here's the honest trade-off. A pallet isn't all cargo. Its own footprint, its height, and the air gaps between stacked cartons all waste space. Loading loose cartons usually fits more into the same container than the identical cartons on pallets.

When loose cartons win

If you're shipping a small, mixed order and just want maximum cartons in the box, loose is hard to beat. The calculator will show you that arrangement — every carton nudged against its neighbor with no hollow space to spare.

When pallets are the right call anyway

But maximum volume isn't always the actual goal. If the destination unloads by forklift, if your goods need to stay off the floor, or if your buyer's warehouse is set up for pallets, the lost space is worth it. You're buying speed and handling safety back with a bit of volume.

How to decide with your real numbers

Forget gut instinct — run both. Put your carton size and weight into the Container Loading Calculator, see the loose-carton count, then account for pallets and compare. The gap between the two is the exact price you pay for the convenience of pallets, and now you can decide if it's worth it.
