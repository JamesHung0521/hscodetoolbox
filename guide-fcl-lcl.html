FCL vs LCL: Which Saves You More?

Practical guidance from HS Code Toolbox

Should you book a full container or ship your goods in a shared one? The answer is rarely about the unit price of a box — it is about your volume, your timeline and how much you value control.

FCL — full container load

With FCL you book an entire container (20ft, 40ft or 40ft high-cube). Your goods fill it, it travels with your consignment, and you get the fastest transit with the least handling.

  • Pros: your cargo is not mixed, fewer touches, usually faster transit, per-unit cost drops sharply once the container is reasonably full.
  • Cons: you pay for a whole container even if it is half empty — wasted space means wasted money.

LCL — less-than-container load

With LCL your cargo is consolidated with other shippers' goods into one container. You pay only for the volume and weight you occupy.

  • Pros: suited to small orders; you only pay for space used.
  • Cons: extra handling at the consolidation warehouse and destination depot, higher per-CBM cost, longer lead time, and a slightly greater risk of damage or delays from sharing.

The decision rule

The crossover is roughly around a third to half a full container. If your cargo fills more than about 40ft-equivalent of a 20ft box, FCL usually wins on both speed and unit cost. Below that, LCL lets you ship small without paying for empty space.

Fixed destination charges (documentation, customs, depot fees) are often similar for both, so at larger volumes they dilute — pushing the balance further toward FCL.

Start with your numbers

Do not guess. Total up your CBM and weight first. If you are near a container's capacity, the FCL route is usually the better deal. If your order is small, LCL is the flexible option.

Frequently asked questions

At what CBM does FCL become cheaper than LCL?

The crossover point varies by trade lane, but in practice it sits somewhere around 10 to 14 CBM for a 20ft container. Below that threshold, LCL per-CBM rates look attractive. Above it, you're paying for space you don't use in a half-full LCL consignment, and the FCL flat rate starts winning. Always ask your forwarder for both quotes side by side before deciding.

Can I mix FCL and LCL for the same order?

Not for the same shipment — each bill of lading is either FCL or LCL. But you can split a large order across multiple shipments, sending the bulk as FCL and a smaller portion as LCL if timing allows. In practice, most shippers just wait until they have enough volume for a full container rather than paying LCL premiums on a partial load.

Is LCL more likely to get damaged than FCL?

Statistically, yes — your cargo shares a container with other shippers' goods, gets handled more times at the consolidation warehouse, and sits in the container longer while it waits to be filled. Fragile or high-value goods almost always do better in FCL. For sturdy, well-palletised commodity goods, the risk difference is small but real.

How much longer does LCL take compared to FCL?

On top of the ocean transit time (which is the same), LCL typically adds 5 to 10 days for consolidation at origin and de-consolidation at destination. That's the warehouse time — waiting for enough cargo to fill the container, then sorting it out at the other end. If your buyer is counting on a specific delivery date, factor that in.

Can I switch from LCL to FCL mid-shipment if my order grows?

Not once the shipment is in motion. But if you haven't booked yet and your volume estimate has grown, absolutely — call your forwarder before the cargo reaches the consolidation warehouse. The earlier you make the switch, the fewer costs you waste on LCL handling that you no longer need.

Does LCL have the same customs documentation requirements as FCL?

Yes. The documentation — commercial invoice, packing list, bill of lading, customs declaration — is the same regardless of whether you ship FCL or LCL. The only difference is that your LCL bill of lading will show a house B/L from the consolidator and a master B/L for the whole container. Make sure your documents match the house B/L, not the master.
