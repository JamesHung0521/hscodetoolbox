HS Code 101

How to Find the Correct HS Code for Your Product in 3 Steps

Practical guidance from HS Code Toolbox

Finding the right HS code does not require a customs expert — it requires a disciplined three-step approach. Do it in this order and you will land on the right classification far more often than guessing.

Step 1 — Start with the product, not the code

Describe your product in plain terms, then search by that name in a lookup tool. Search by what the product is and what it is made of — the HS system classifies by composition and function, not by brand.

Step 2 — Read the subheading wording carefully

Open the candidate subheadings and compare their exact wording to your product. Customs classifies by the legal text of the description, not by what the item is commonly called. Watch for words like 'of plastics', 'of iron or steel', or 'parts and accessories', which change the classification.

Step 3 — Verify with the chapter notes and a broker

Read the section and chapter notes for your candidate code — they contain crucial inclusions and exclusions. Then, for high-value or ambiguous products, confirm the code with your customs broker before committing.

A wrong code can change your duty rate, trigger exams, or cause delays. The small effort of double-checking is worth it.

Frequently asked questions

Can I just use ChatGPT to find my HS code?

You can get a starting point from AI tools, but never ship on an AI-generated code without verifying it against the official tariff schedule. AI doesn't read chapter notes or legal exclusions — the things that actually determine classification. Use it for a rough idea, then confirm with official sources or a broker.

How long does it usually take to classify a product?

For a straightforward product with a clear description, you're looking at 15 to 30 minutes of focused lookup. Composite goods or products with multiple functions can take an hour or more. If you're doing this for dozens of SKUs, budget a few hours and work through them systematically rather than rushing.

What if my product fits under two different headings?

That's a genuine classification dilemma, and it happens more often than you'd think. The HS system has General Rules of Interpretation (GIRs) that tell you which heading wins — usually the one with the most specific description, or the one that gives the product its essential character. If you can't resolve it yourself, a binding ruling from customs is the safest move.

Do I need a different HS code for export and import?

The first 6 digits are the same worldwide, so the base code doesn't change between export and import. But the full national extension (8 or 10 digits) may differ because each country adds its own subdivisions. Your export declaration uses your origin country's code; your import declaration uses the destination country's code.

Should I pay a broker to classify my products?

If you're shipping regularly and the products are anything beyond commodity basics, yes — a licensed customs broker's classification is worth the fee. They carry professional liability and their code choices hold up under audit. For one-off personal shipments, a lookup tool and common sense usually get you there.

Is there a free way to verify my HS code?

Yes. Most major markets publish their tariff schedules online for free — the US ITC for HTSUS, the EU TARIC database, and China's customs portal. Cross-referencing your candidate code against two or three of these is a solid free verification step before you commit.
