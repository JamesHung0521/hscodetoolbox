About

Tools built from the factory floor

I built HS Code Toolbox out of a frustration I know too well. I run a metal giftware factory that has been exporting since before the iPhone existed, and for over 20 years I've watched good shipments get held up over things that should have been simple — a wrong HS code, a container that came back overweight, an invoice field that didn't match the packing list.

The tools that should have solved this were either built for shipping agents, buried behind sign-up walls, or so jargon-heavy they needed a degree to use. So I made my own: free, no sign-up, and written for the person actually packing and shipping the goods — because that's who I am.

Everything here answers the day-to-day questions that come up when you're about to ship: what HS code do I use, which container fits my cargo, and am I about to overload it? Our guides come from hands-on exporting experience, and we deliberately prefer real numbers and real scenarios over theoretical fluff.

Our dual engine

Tools + content. Quick calculators and lookups for instant answers, backed by a knowledge base that explains the "why" — so you can make better, more confident shipping decisions.

A note on accuracy

Trade rules, duty rates and codes change. Our tools are provided for reference and should always be cross-checked with your customs broker or the official tariff of the destination country.

Who's behind this site

Hi, I'm James, and I run that factory. Over the years I've exported mainly to Europe and North America, and I still handle the questions that actually decide whether a shipment makes its sailing: finding the right HS code, and packing a container that won't bounce back overweight. I'm not a marketing team or a content agency writing about trade from the outside — I'm on the operations side, dealing with customs declarations, loading plans and deadline pressure on every order.

That background shapes everything here. The tools and guides favour real numbers and real scenarios over theory, because they come from the operations side of the business — customs declarations, loading plans and deadline pressure — rather than from a textbook. When I say "check with your broker," I mean it, because I have seen how often the small details change the finished rate.
