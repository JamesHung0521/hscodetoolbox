FCL vs LCL: Which Saves You More?

Practical guidance from HS Code Toolbox

Should you book a full container or ship your goods in a shared one? The answer is rarely about the unit price of a box — it is about your volume, your timeline and how much you value control.

FCL — full container load

With FCL you book an entire container (20ft, 40ft or 40ft high-cube). Your goods fill it, it travels with your consignment, and you get the fastest transit with the least handling.

  • Pros: your cargo is not mixed, fewer touches, usually faster transit, per-unit cost drops sharply once the container is reasonably full.
  • Cons: you pay for a whole container even if it is half empty — wasted space means wasted money.

LCL — less-than-container load

With LCL your cargo is consolidated with other shippers' goods into one container. You pay only for the volume and weight you occupy.

  • Pros: suited to small orders; you only pay for space used.
  • Cons: extra handling at the consolidation warehouse and destination depot, higher per-CBM cost, longer lead time, and a slightly greater risk of damage or delays from sharing.

The decision rule

The crossover is roughly around a third to half a full container. If your cargo fills more than about 40ft-equivalent of a 20ft box, FCL usually wins on both speed and unit cost. Below that, LCL lets you ship small without paying for empty space.

Fixed destination charges (documentation, customs, depot fees) are often similar for both, so at larger volumes they dilute — pushing the balance further toward FCL.

Start with your numbers

Do not guess. Total up your CBM and weight first. If you are near a container's capacity, the FCL route is usually the better deal. If your order is small, LCL is the flexible option.
