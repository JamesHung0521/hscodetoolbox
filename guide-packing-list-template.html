Packing List Template — Why It Exists and How to Fill It

Practical guidance from HS Code Toolbox

The packing list is the field guide to your cargo. While the invoice tells customs what you are shipping and what it's worth, the packing list tells them exactly how it's packed — how many cartons, how heavy, how much volume. Customs and the carrier check them together, and a mismatch between the two is one of the most common reasons a hold happens.

The template

PACKING LIST Ref.: ____ · Date: ____
Company name, address, contact
Company name, address, country
The actual container and seal numbers once loaded
The case/carton marks matching the invoice
e.g. 200 cartons / 8 pallets
Same wording as the invoice — no shortcuts
Units per carton and total across all packages
Per package and total, in kg — be exact
Carton size L×W×H and total volume

Why every line matters

  • Counts and marks are what the terminal physically checks against. If the marks on the cartons do not match the list, the cargo looks unverifiable and gets flagged.
  • Weights feed the VGM (verified gross mass) and the overweight limits. A packing list that understates weight is exactly how an overweight container slips through until it is too late.
  • Measurement (CBM) lets the carrier allocate space and tells you — before you book — which container fits and whether you are volume-bound or weight-bound.
  • Consistency with the invoice is the single biggest check. If the description or HS code differs between the two documents, customs treats it as a red flag even when it was an honest error.

The quick checklist

  • One packing list per shipment, matching the invoice line for line.
  • Exact weights and counts — never "approx".
  • Same goods description and HS code as the commercial invoice.
  • Total CBM computed before you book, so you pick the right container the first time.
