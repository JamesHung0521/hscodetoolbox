How Many Cartons Fit in a 20ft Container? Real Loading Math

Practical guidance from HS Code Toolbox

Everybody asks this, and the honest answer is "it depends." But unlike the internet's magic numbers, that answer is actually calculable. Here's how to work out your own real number.

What a 20ft actually holds

A 20ft container has about 33 cubic metres of space and a payload around 28,000 kg. The practical usable volume is a bit less — call it 85–90% of nominal — because of the door corrugations and the way cartons rarely fill a box perfectly.

The quick estimate by volume

Take a 60cm × 40cm × 30cm carton: that's 0.6 × 0.4 × 0.3 = 0.072 m³. Using roughly 30 m³ of usable space, that gives about 30 / 0.072 ≈ 415 cartons by volume alone.

Then weight cuts it down

Never stop at volume. If each of those 415 cartons is 25 kg, that's 415 × 25 ≈ 10 tonnes — well under the 28,000 kg payload, so for this light example volume is the boss. But pack denser goods and the weight limit takes over instead. You have to check both.

Orientation changes the count

How each carton sits — upright, on its side, flat — changes how many slide into the 20ft's length, width and height. A proper calculation tries every orientation and keeps the best. That's the part back-of-envelope math gets wrong.

Rather than guess, run your actual carton through the calculator — it tests all orientations against volume and weight for the 20ft and the others, and gives you the real number. Free, no sign-up.
