Customs Clearance 101: The Documents You Need for Every Shipment

Practical guidance from HS Code Toolbox

Two thirds of clearance delays come down to paperwork. Forgetting one document — or one field — can stop a shipment at the border. Here is the core set every export shipment needs, in plain language.

The indispensable core

Commercial Invoice: the value and description of the goods, buyer and seller, terms of sale, and HS codes. Its accuracy sets the whole clearance in motion.

Packing List: what is in each package, weights and dimensions. Essential for the container loading and for customs to match the shipment.

Bill of Lading (B/L): the transport document and evidence of the contract of carriage — and often the document of title. You cannot release the cargo without it.

Documents that depend on your goods and market

Certificate of Origin (or a preferential origin form under a trade agreement) to claim reduced or zero duty.

For regulated goods: export/import licenses, phytosanitary certificates for wood or plants, health certificates for food, and certificates of conformity or safety. Check your product and destination specifically.

Why the HS code matters here

The HS code connects your invoice to the duties, licenses and restrictions that apply. A wrong code can trigger an exam, a re-valuation, or a penalty at a time when you cannot afford a delay.

Getting the code right at the quoting stage — before the paperwork is created — saves you from a costly re-issue down the line.

A simple habit

Build a checklist per shipment and review it against the destination's import requirements. The exporters who clear faster are rarely the clever ones — just the best organized.
